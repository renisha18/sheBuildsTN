When friends first started ordering cakes from me, I charged whatever felt polite. It took a few months to notice that I was often paying to bake for other people.

## Counting the hidden costs

I began writing down everything that went into an order: ingredients, gas, packaging, the drive to deliver it and the hours spent decorating. Time was the cost I had never counted, and it was the biggest one.

With that list in front of me, setting a price became a calculation rather than a guess. I added a little extra for the batches that do not turn out as planned.

Raising prices was uncomfortable, but most regular customers understood. The ones who stayed valued the work, and I could finally enjoy it again.
